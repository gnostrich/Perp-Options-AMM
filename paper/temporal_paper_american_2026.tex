\begin{abstract}
Perpetual options let perpetual-futures traders cap liquidation risk without
closing their positions. Existing venues price them with complex machinery and
fragment liquidity across strikes. We show that the entire out-of-the-money
(OTM) strike continuum, for both calls and puts, can be priced in closed form by
a \emph{single} liquidity pool built on the weighted constant-product
(``Balancer'') invariant $x^{w}\,y^{1-w}=k$ with one dynamic weight $w$. Trades
\emph{skew the curve} rather than sliding the reserves point along a fixed curve;
the reachable states form the trajectory hyperbola $(x-\alpha)(y-\beta)=\alpha\beta$,
tangent to the pricing curve at the reserves point. This draft generalises the
prior \emph{barrier} instrument (whose value saturates at one perpetual-future
unit when in-the-money) into an \emph{American-style} perpetual option: a holder's
optimal exercise is governed by a closed-form smooth-pasting free boundary
$S^{\ast}=K\,\gamma/(\gamma+1)$, with no new parameters and a $C^{1}$ seam between
the continuation region and the intrinsic payoff. A separate, vol-calibrated
\emph{constant slope-multiplier} lens $m$ acts as a kurtosis knob, setting the
option-value steepness to $m\gamma$ at every strike. The settlement, free
boundary, no-arbitrage biconditional, and curve/lens spine have been
machine-checked by the Aristotle theorem prover (\tfp{}; not independently
re-verified against a local kernel). We are deliberate about scope: replication
is exact only for delta magnitude $|\Gamma|\le 1$ (a single-wing bound), with
$|\Gamma|>1$ treated as a labelled approximation; and pool \emph{solvency}
remains \emph{conditional} on an external funding port that is shown to be
necessary but is not discharged here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and contributions}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

A perpetual future lets a trader hold leveraged directional exposure with no
expiry, at the cost of a discontinuous, ruinous \emph{liquidation} when
collateral is exhausted. A perpetual option on the same underlying---a call to
hedge a short, a put to hedge a long---caps realised loss beyond a chosen strike
and, because it does not expire, holds that protection for as long as the
underlying perp is held. The pricing of such a continuum of strikes is the
problem this paper addresses.

The prior version of this work~\cite{temporal-barrier} priced the OTM continuum
with a single weighted constant-product pool and treated each position as a
\emph{barrier} instrument: its value rises toward one perpetual-future unit as
spot approaches the strike, then is capped at that ceiling once in-the-money and
held there. This draft keeps that entire pricing spine and \emph{lifts} the
barrier ceiling into an American-style \emph{early-exercise free boundary}. The
ceiling becomes the point at which optimal exercise occurs; continuation runs
\emph{past} the strike to a formula-determined boundary and then pays intrinsic.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item A single pool on the Balancer invariant $x^{w}y^{1-w}=k$ that prices the
        whole OTM call/put strike continuum in closed form, where \emph{trades
        skew the curve} (the weight $w$ is the moving quantity), reachable states
        lie on the trajectory hyperbola, and pricing is faithful because the two
        curves are tangent at the reserves point (Section~\ref{sec:amm}).
  \item The \emph{American generalisation}: a closed-form smooth-pasting free
        boundary $S^{\ast}=K\,\gamma/(\gamma+1)$ with $C^{1}$ value/slope match
        and no new parameters, replacing the barrier ceiling
        (Section~\ref{sec:american}). The boundary and its $C^{1}$ join are
        machine-checked by the Aristotle prover (\tfp{}).
  \item A vol-calibrated \emph{constant slope-multiplier} lens $m$ acting as a
        kurtosis knob: it fixes the option-value steepness to $m\gamma$
        \emph{uniformly across strikes}, with $m=1$ recovering the plain
        Balancer curve (Section~\ref{sec:lens}). The pool flow itself is
        unchanged by the lens.
  \item A structural no-internal-arbitrage result: a costless collar yields zero
        capital-efficiency surplus \emph{if and only if} the pool is symmetric
        ($w=\tfrac12$), a genuine biconditional with an explicit skew
        counterexample (Section~\ref{sec:noarb}), machine-checked by Aristotle
        (\tfp{}).
  \item An honest \emph{formal-verification ledger} (Section~\ref{sec:formal})
        that states exactly what is machine-checked, under what provenance label,
        and what remains the open frontier---namely, that \emph{solvency is
        conditional} on a funding port shown necessary but not discharged.
\end{enumerate}

\section{American-style perpetual options}\label{sec:perp}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Definition}
Perpetual options are non-expiring contracts-for-difference representing the
payoff from favourable price action---upward for calls, downward for
puts---of an underlying asset relative to a denominating asset (USD here)
beyond a strike $K$. In the prior draft the instrument was a \emph{barrier}: its
value topped out at one perpetual-future unit when in-the-money (ITM) and stayed
at that ceiling. Here the instrument is \emph{American-style}: while the holder
remains out-of-the-money the position carries continuation value; once spot moves
far enough in the holder's favour, optimal exercise pays the intrinsic value
measured from the strike. The transition is not at the strike itself but at a
free boundary determined below (Section~\ref{sec:american}).

\subsection{Utility}
Liquidation in a perpetual future is the involuntary termination of a leveraged
position when collateral is exhausted. Composing a perp with an OTM perpetual
option on the same underlying caps realised loss past the strike, bounding the
trader's drawdown without forcing position closure; because the option does not
expire, the hedge persists for the life of the perp. The American generalisation
makes the protected leg behave like a genuine early-exercisable claim rather than
a hard barrier, which is what a perpetual liquidation hedge economically is:
there is no expiry to wait for, so the holder's only decision is \emph{when} the
protection is best realised.

\subsection{Pricing: the mark, with no separate premium}
For an OTM strike the value of a position is its \emph{mark}: the lesser of the
strike-ray slope and its reciprocal,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{mark}=\min\!\bigl(\mathrm{slope},\,1/\mathrm{slope}\bigr)\in(0,1],
  \label{eq:mark}
\end{equation}
a dimensionless quantity read as the \emph{fraction of a full perpetual future}
the position is worth: it approaches $1$ near the money and decays toward $0$
deep OTM. The value of a position of size $q$ is $q\cdot\mathrm{mark}$.

This pricing carries \emph{no separate premium object}. The mark is the complete
description of what a position is worth; there is no extrinsic/intrinsic
decomposition layered on top, and ``the value of a position'' means
$q\cdot\mathrm{mark}$ and nothing else. The American generalisation does not
introduce a premium either: it only changes how the mark is computed once spot
moves past the strike (continuation through the strike, then intrinsic), as
Section~\ref{sec:american} makes precise.

\section{The AMM construction}\label{sec:amm}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

We make two generalisations to the constant-function AMM $x^{w}y^{1-w}=k$ that
give it the expressivity to price perpetual options across the continuum. Both
are inherited unchanged from the prior draft.

\subsection{Trades skew the curve}
\emph{``Trades skew the AMM curve instead of moving the reserves point along
it.''} A two-asset constant-function market maker (CFMM) is a curve in the
$(x,y)$ plane along which the reserves $(x,y)$ are a single point; the price is
the slope there, and an ordinary trade slides the reserves point along the curve.

Two curves are in play. The \textbf{Balancer curve} $x^{w}y^{1-w}=k$ has its
\emph{shape} set by the weight $w$; this is the curve that skews. The
\textbf{trajectory hyperbola}
\begin{equation}
  (x-\alpha)(y-\beta)=\alpha\beta,\qquad \alpha=x\,w,\quad \beta=y\,(1-w),
  \label{eq:hyperbola}
\end{equation}
is the locus the reserves point actually travels. The two are tangent at the
reserves point---they share a slope there---so pricing read off the Balancer
curve is faithful, and ``reshaping the curve'' and ``moving along the
trajectory'' describe the same event from two angles. Rather than re-quote prices
by moving the reserves point along a fixed curve, the pool obtains the same
effect by reshaping the Balancer curve via $w$.

\subsection{Trades happen anywhere on the curve}
In ordinary CFMMs trades are spot swaps, always at the reserves point. We treat
spot swaps as the special case of perpetual-option swaps, which may occur at
\emph{any} OTM point on the curve. Each ray from the origin, parameterised by the
ratio $y/x$, is identified with a unique strike; the ray meets the curve at one
\emph{trade point}, and a transaction at that point is handled as if the trade
point were the reserves point. The $45^{\circ}$ ray ($y=x$) is the at-the-money
strike; rays steeper and shallower partition the OTM continuum into the call and
put wings, both priced from the same $(x,y,w)$ state.

\subsection{Conservation law and trade formula}
For each trade the quantities $\alpha=xw$ and $\beta=y(1-w)$ are individually
conserved, so the pool state is fully determined by $(x,y)$, with $w=\alpha/x$ a
derived field. The conservation law confines reachable states to the trajectory
hyperbola~\eqref{eq:hyperbola}. Parameterising a swap by its cash leg $\Delta y$
(cash into the pool, negative if out),
\begin{equation}
  y'=y+\Delta y,\qquad
  \Delta x=-\frac{\alpha\beta\,\Delta y}{(y-\beta)(y'-\beta)},\qquad
  \Delta w=\frac{\beta\,\Delta y}{y\,y'},
  \label{eq:trade}
\end{equation}
with the post-trade state $(x',y',w')$ preserving $\alpha,\beta$ by construction.
The third update is not independent: since $w=\alpha/x$ and $\alpha$ is conserved,
$\Delta w$ is fixed by $\Delta x$ (it is the simplification of
$\alpha/x'-\alpha/x$); it is written out because $w$ is the quantity the pool's
pricing actually moves. A leg's notional $q$ is range-invariant: adjusting a
position's strike range reshapes its value and its cash leg but not $q$.

The conservation law, the $w$-skew trade rule, and the tangency that makes
pricing faithful are machine-checked: see Section~\ref{sec:formal}
(LENSKERNEL \texttt{tradeUpdate\_alpha/beta/hyperbola}, the constant-$m$
\texttt{TemporalAMM} \texttt{invariant}/\texttt{trade\_dx} spine; \tfp{}).

\subsection{Carry, strike normalisation, and funding}
The pool carries a reference price $P=N_y/N_x$ with $u=\log(\mathrm{price})-\log
P$. Sustained directional drift would otherwise decouple pool spot from oracle
spot; \emph{strike normalisation} periodically \emph{rebases} the frame by a
factor $r$ ($x\to rx$, $\alpha\to r\alpha$, $k\to r^{w}k$, strike ray
$\theta\to\theta/r$), bounding drift without nullifying the standing deviation
from the anchor curve. Trades and rebases \emph{commute}, so the pricing frame is
well defined independent of interleaving (machine-checked: PH6
\texttt{R\_form\_rebase\_invariant} and the constant-$m$
\texttt{trade\_rebase\_commute}; \tfp{}).

\emph{Funding} anchors pool spot to oracle spot. By analogy with a perpetual
future, a position accrues funding according to the deviation of the live pool
curve from the anchor curve \emph{at that position's own strike ray}: the
magnitude is the size of the per-ray deviation and the direction follows the
perp analogy (if the pool prices the option above its anchor value, the long side
pays). Funding is therefore countercyclical---the crowded side pays the
contrarian side---and a rebase bounds the rate at which drift accumulates into
the deviation that funding prices.

\section{The constant slope-multiplier lens (the kurtosis knob)}\label{sec:lens}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The pool above prices coherently within one fixed liquidity shape. The prior
draft identified the second free moment---\emph{kurtosis}, the peakedness of the
liquidity distribution---as a direction for future work. This draft realises it
as a \emph{constant slope-multiplier} lens.

\subsection{Definition}
Let $\gamma>1$ be the curve's convexity exponent, so that option value follows
the power law $\mathrm{value}\propto S^{-\gamma}$ in the wings. The lens
introduces a single scalar $m>0$ and sets the \emph{lensed} option-value
exponent
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mathrm{loc}}(K)=m\,\gamma\qquad\text{for every strike }K,
  \label{eq:lens}
\end{equation}
i.e. the steepness is multiplied by $m$ \emph{uniformly across strikes}. Setting
$m=1$ gives $g_{\mathrm{loc}}=\gamma$, the plain Balancer curve; larger $m$ makes
the option-value curve steeper everywhere, and the corresponding trade-point map
$\theta_{\mathrm{tx}}=\mathrm{mode}\cdot(\mathrm{chosen}/\mathrm{mode})^{m}$
places a trade further out. Steepness and outward placement therefore move in the
\emph{same} direction with $m$.

\paragraph{What the lens is and is not.}
The lens is \emph{vol-calibrated at setup and static under trading}: it is the
curve's geometry chosen so the pricing surface suits an asset of a given
volatility, not a trader-measured statistic. Crucially the lens leaves the
\textbf{pool flow unchanged}: the trade rule~\eqref{eq:trade}, the
oracle-arbitrage map, and the rebase are the plain Balancer dynamics; $x$, $y$,
and $w$ move exactly as in Section~\ref{sec:amm}. The lens is a forward read
applied to everything \emph{read} (pricing, the option chart, settlement,
funding, portfolio value) and \emph{written} (trades, settle-at-strike) through
one shared helper; it is never inverted, and the underlying pool stays plain
Balancer. The pool-byte-identity and the $g_{\mathrm{loc}}=m\gamma$ properties
are machine-checked (constant-$m$ \texttt{TemporalAMM}: \texttt{g\_eq\_m\_gamma},
\texttt{g\_const\_in\_strike}, \texttt{g\_eq\_gamma\_iff\_m\_one},
\texttt{warp\_linear} $\int m = m\,\Delta\gamma$; \tfp{}).

\subsection{The lens's natural home (scoped reading, not load-bearing)}
\label{sec:gibbs}
There is a clean reading of $m$ as an inverse temperature, which we include only
with its scope stated. In the \emph{wings}, where value follows the power law
$\mathrm{value}(S)=S^{-m\gamma}=e^{-(m\gamma)\,q}$ with $q=\log S$, the value is
a Gibbs weight with inverse temperature $\beta_{T}=m\gamma$, so
$\mathrm{value}_{m}=(\mathrm{value}_{1})^{m}$ and ``colder'' means ``steeper.''
This is \emph{wing-scoped}: it describes the power-law tail, not the engine's
\emph{bounded smooth-pasted mark} near the money (where the measured
mark-to-$S^{-g}$ ratio departs from unity by orders of magnitude). $m$ acts as a
dilation $\gamma\mapsto m\gamma$, not as a measure tilt. This reading is itself
machine-checked as a self-contained statement (LENS\_THERMAL:
\texttt{value\_is\_gibbs}, \texttt{value\_pow\_m},
\texttt{invtemp\_eq\_m\_gamma}, \texttt{m\_one\_recovers\_base}; \tfp{}) but is
\emph{not load-bearing} for the construction: the engine's option value is the
bounded smooth-pasted mark, not the bare power law. We note, for completeness and
without claiming more, that a \emph{measure-backed} information geometry
(exponential family / Fisher metric of a probability measure) is \emph{not}
available for the Balancer curve; only a measure-free Hessian / dually-flat
geometry survives.

\section{American-style exercise: the smooth-pasting free boundary}
\label{sec:american}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the new core of the paper: the barrier ceiling of the prior draft becomes
an early-exercise free boundary in closed form, with no new parameters.

\subsection{The construction}
The continuation value is the curve value $c\cdot s_{N}$ (the curve gives value
$\propto s_{N}\propto S^{-\gamma}$, where $s_{N}$ is the normalised strike
coordinate). It pastes onto the strike-intrinsic payoff at a free boundary fixed
by \emph{smooth-pasting}---value-match and slope-match, two conditions in two
unknowns. The solution is closed-form:
\begin{align}
  s_{N}^{\ast} &= \theta\Bigl(\tfrac{\gamma+1}{\gamma}\Bigr)^{\!\gamma},
  & \text{equivalently}\quad
  S^{\ast} &= K\,\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1},
  \label{eq:boundary}\\
  c &= \frac{1}{(\gamma+1)\,s_{N}^{\ast}},
  & \text{value fraction at the boundary} &= \frac{1}{\gamma+1}.
  \label{eq:coeff}
\end{align}
Here $\theta=s_{N}(K)$ is the strike registered in the curve's carry coordinate;
because $\theta\to\theta/r$ on rebase, $s_{N}^{\ast}$ tracks the strike
automatically. The mark reaches its cap of $1$ only at \emph{full} exercise, not
at the boundary: at $S^{\ast}$ the position is worth the fraction $1/(\gamma+1)$.

\subsection{The new mark / exercise rule}
Per leg, wing-aware, with $s_{N}=s_{N}(S)$ and $s_{N}^{\ast},c$ as above:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Continuation} (on the OTM side of $s_{N}^{\ast}$):
        $\mathrm{mark}=c\cdot s_{N}$. This runs \emph{through} the old strike ray;
        the strike is now an ordinary interior point with no saturation there.
  \item \textbf{Exercise} (past $s_{N}^{\ast}$): $\mathrm{mark}=\text{intrinsic
        from the strike}$. On the bounded (vertical-spread) wing the intrinsic is
        $1-S/K$, reaching $1$ at full exercise; on the unbounded (single-option)
        wing the intrinsic keeps growing past $1$ and is \emph{not} capped (an
        uncapped option is correct; bounding comes from using a spread).
  \item \textbf{Trigger}: the ratio $\text{intrinsic}/\text{continuation}$ reaches
        $1$ exactly at $s_{N}^{\ast}$, so that crossing is the exercise switch and
        it lands at the formula point, not at the money.
\end{itemize}
This generalises the barrier rule of the prior draft. There, the mark saturated
to $1$ at the strike and stayed at the ceiling once ITM; here the ceiling is
lifted into the free boundary~\eqref{eq:boundary}, and the closed-form
continuation runs past the strike before exercise pays intrinsic. The curve
functions, calibration, and funding are unchanged by the rule---it lives entirely
in the per-leg mark.

\subsection{Why this is the American boundary}
The smooth-paste solution~\eqref{eq:boundary}--\eqref{eq:coeff} is not an
arbitrary $C^{1}$ join. It is the \emph{unique} solution of the value-match plus
slope-match system, and it coincides with the optimal early-exercise boundary in
the variational sense. Both facts are machine-checked: the boundary is
\emph{generated} as the unique smooth-pasting solution (T1a
\texttt{Sstar\_A\_forced}, \texttt{coeffA\_forced}; \tfp{}), and $S^{\ast}$
maximises the holder's value over \emph{deterministic} exercise boundaries (T1b
\texttt{opt\_boundary\_is\_max\_A}, a genuine variational result, \tfp{}). The
identification with the stochastic optimal-stopping (Snell) boundary is
\emph{named but not formalised}---the \texttt{AmericanOptimalityPrinciple} field
is a placeholder obligation (\texttt{True}), not a discharged premise. The closed-form boundary also sits inside a
deeper tie to the classical perpetual-American (Merton) problem: $\gamma$ is the
characteristic root of the underlying Laplace exponent, the relation
$\gamma(\gamma+1)=2r/\sigma^{2}$ recovers the Gaussian (Merton) slice, and
$S^{\ast}$ is the Merton boundary on that slice (MERTON tie
\texttt{Sstar\_is\_merton\_boundary}, \texttt{gaussian\_limit\_quadratic};
\tfp{}, with the GH/Bessel-K distributional layer carried as named hypotheses).

\subsection{The $C^{1}$ seam, machine-checked two ways}
At $s_{N}^{\ast}$ the continuation and intrinsic must agree in value \emph{and}
slope. This is checked two ways.
\emph{Formally:} the value-match and slope-match conditions are
machine-checked at the boundary for every steepness $g>0$ (R1
\texttt{valueMatch\_A}/\texttt{slopeMatch\_A}; LENSKERNEL
\texttt{valueMatch\_g}/\texttt{slopeMatch\_g} for arbitrary $g$, hence directly at
$g=m\gamma$; constant-$m$ \texttt{paste\_value}/\texttt{paste\_slope}; \tfp{}),
and the OTM$\to$ITM crossover lands at the dollar strike $K$ for all $\gamma$
under the registration $\theta=s_{N}(K)$ (R2 \texttt{crossover\_sNorm\_at\_K};
\tfp{}).
\emph{On the engine:} the live engine satisfies the seam to $0.04\%$ in value and
$0.1\%$ in slope for $\gamma\in\{1.5,2,3,4\}$ (the $0.1\%$ is the curve table's
$S^{-\gamma}$ accuracy, not a structural gap), under an acceptance gate of
$\le 0.15\%$ on both with no jump and no kink. A companion no-jump check confirms
the held position's value is continuous across the at-the-money / OTM$\leftrightarrow$ITM
crossing on the live value path (a distinct locus from the settlement seam; this
is an engine gate, not a Lean theorem).

\subsection{The replication scope: $|\Gamma|\le1$ exact, $|\Gamma|>1$ approximate}
\label{sec:gamma}
The construction replicates the target American payoff \emph{exactly} for delta
magnitude $|\Gamma|\le1$, which is a single-wing bound. ``True American'' exercise
and ``exact replication per wing'' are mutually exclusive once $|\Gamma|>1$, so we
state $|\Gamma|\le1$ as the exact regime and treat $|\Gamma|>1$ as a
\emph{labelled approximation}. We do not claim exact replication outside the
$|\Gamma|\le1$ regime.

\section{Settlement}\label{sec:settlement}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

A perpetual-option position is opened as a \emph{band}---a composition of option
legs---against a specific perpetual future the trader already holds, the
\emph{origin perp}. When the band is opened it is minted against a carved slice of
that aggregated perp; the slice's absolute notional, equity, and entry mark are
frozen for the life of the band, so that settlement attribution cannot be
contaminated by unrelated perp activity. Settlement is therefore
\emph{perp-denominated}: leg values are carried in units of the carved perp and
converted to cash at the slice's closing equity, with the band's frozen leverage
$L_{0}$ amplifying the settled net and a counterparty-equity floor as a final
guard.

Closing and exercising are economically equivalent for the holder. Each piece of
a position carries its \emph{original strike} (as opened) and an \emph{effective
strike} that equals the original while OTM. Under the American rule the effective
strike is simply the original strike throughout---continuation and exercise are
both referenced to $K$---so the closing swap returns the position's full value,
intrinsic included when exercised, with no separate exercise path. The
composite-ray closed form for settlement holds across the OTM-to-ITM boundary
under this substitution (machine-checked: C1
\texttt{compositeRay\_ITM\_substitution}; \tfp{}). A band has two legs on opposite
wings, and since spot cannot be ITM on both at once, at most one leg is ITM at any
time; settlement then has a clean two-case structure (both legs reversed on the
AMM when neither is ITM; the ITM leg settled to cash by the closed form and the
OTM leg reversed when one is). The stage-2 mark/exercise change does not alter the
stage-3 carved-perp$\to$dollar conversion, which applies uniformly to continuation
and exercised legs.

\section{No internal arbitrage}\label{sec:noarb}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Within a single pool state $(x,y,w)$, no composition of swaps across rays yields a
riskless capital-efficiency edge. The sharp statement concerns the \emph{costless
collar}: a band whose sold and bought legs are arranged to be cash-neutral by
construction.

\begin{proposition}[Collar surplus vanishes exactly at symmetry]\label{prop:collar}
The costless-collar capital-efficiency surplus is zero \emph{if and only if} the
pool is symmetric, i.e. $w=\tfrac12$. Under skew ($w\ne\tfrac12$) the surplus is
non-zero, with an explicit counterexample.
\end{proposition}

The biconditional is the substance: the result discriminates between symmetric and
skewed pools, which is suggestive that it reflects structure rather than being purely
an artifact of the chosen coordinates (the Lean \texttt{collarSurplus} is a posited
coordinate; see the carried-form caveat below). The edge is a \emph{skew phenomenon}---it exists only when the
pool is directionally skewed and vanishes exactly at symmetry. The statement is a
claim about \emph{pricing}, not about the pool being left unchanged: a costless
collar's two legs transact in the same direction against the curve, so the pool
does move ($w$ shifts), but the trader pays for that displacement at fair,
sequenced prices (the proceeds of a swap are the path integral of the marginal
price along the curve), so there is no free cash to extract. Liquidation
protection retains genuine risk-management value even at zero skew; zero skew
removes the \emph{free} capital-efficiency edge, not the \emph{worth} of the
protection.

Proposition~\ref{prop:collar} is machine-checked (C2
\texttt{collarSurplus\_zero\_iff\_half}; \tfp{}, with the surplus form carried as
a named definition). A companion result formalises the no-arbitrage symmetry as a
reflection: a put is the reflected call ($\theta^{2}/s$), so no-arbitrage is the
curve's symmetry (C3 \texttt{reflection\_arrow}; \tfp{}). We are precise about its
status: the reflection \emph{arrow} is a proved identity, but the link from the
spec mark to the live engine's instrument remains a residual modeling premise (the
engine-faithfulness question), so C3 is best read as a \emph{conditional skeleton}
rather than an unconditional no-arbitrage theorem over the production engine.

\subsection{Provenance discipline}
All Lean results below were compiled by the Aristotle theorem prover server-side
in the matching toolchain (Lean~4.28.0 / Mathlib~v4.28.0) and passed a zero-cost
artifact audit: a forbidden-token scan (no \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{admit}/
\texttt{native\_decide}/\texttt{sorryAx}/\texttt{opaque}/\texttt{unsafe}), the
prover-reported \texttt{\#print axioms} restricted to
$\{\texttt{propext},\texttt{Classical.choice},\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$, an

\emph{not} re-run against a local canonical kernel in this work; we therefore
label them \tfp{} and never ``verified.'' A local canonical-env build is the
single label upgrade to ``verified'' and is environment-blocked here. We also
distinguish \emph{grounded} results (proved over the real objects with no
load-bearing carried hypothesis) from \emph{carried} results (conditional on
named structure-field hypotheses that are not discharged, never on \texttt{axiom}
declarations).

\subsection{The conditional-solvency interface stack}
A separate prover line formalises the curve$\to$value$\to$storage interface and
its passivity/solvency guarantees as a typed stack (\texttt{AMMCurve.lean},
\texttt{Temporal.lean}, \texttt{Seam.lean}). It establishes, machine-checked:
(i)~a \emph{curve gate}---a shape failing convexity/monotonicity/coercivity
cannot be constructed, so cannot reach any theorem; (ii)~\emph{short-gamma
universality}---pool value is concave in price for any valid curve
(\texttt{poolValue\_concaveOn}) and this propagates into stored equity
(\texttt{intrinsic\_concaveOn}) by types, not inspection; (iii)~the headline
\texttt{reserves\_have\_no\_floor}: with a convex obligation $O=p^{2}$, reserve
value is unbounded below, so \emph{the solvency floor cannot come from
reserves}---``convexity must be funded,'' as a theorem at the storage interface;
and (iv)~conditional solvency: \emph{given} a port that covers the concave
deficit, a curve-wired pool is passive and solvent for all time with no free lunch
(\texttt{CurvePool.solvent\_forever}). All \tfp{}; the proven instances are
\texttt{cpmm} (equal-weight Balancer frontier) and \texttt{expPool}
(exponential).

\paragraph{The open frontier, stated plainly.}
Solvency is \emph{conditional}. The hypotheses B1 (the port covers the floor), B3
(arb is one-way), and B4 (the ledger closes) are \emph{structure fields}, not
discharged facts. The stack proves ``\emph{if} the ledger closes, arb is one-way,
and the port covers the floor, \emph{then} passivity and solvency''; it does
\emph{not} prove the port pays in the real world, which is the external
funding/hedge assumption. \texttt{reserves\_have\_no\_floor} shows the port is
\emph{necessary}, not \emph{sufficient}: geometry is necessary-never-sufficient for
solvency. We do not claim solvency is closed by the construction.

\paragraph{What the stack does not establish.}
The typed contracts (\texttt{price/tick/ledger/portFlow/arbLeak}) are \emph{not}
wired to the production HTML engine's numeric formulas; the package machine-checks the
interface and its propagation, not that the engine instantiates the fields. That
instantiation (engine formulas $\vdash$ B1/B3/B4) is the next body of work. The
instances are representative, not exhaustive: weighted Balancer and the power-sum
family are not instantiated, only the mechanism that would transfer through the
abstract interface.

\subsection{The whole-exchange ``single object'' (research direction)}
We have a partial result toward unifying the entire exchange---storage, flow,
gauge, funding port, settlement port, dollar pipe, and a parameterised close
mechanic---as a \emph{single} port-Hamiltonian object whose solvency reads as
passivity-under-admissible-inputs. The \emph{internal} half is machine-checked
(\tfp{}): \texttt{exchange\_internal\_passivity}, with the resistive curvature's
nonnegativity discharged \emph{by the geometry} (the proven \texttt{R\_psd}), so
the system is passive \emph{because} the geometry is PSD, plus
\texttt{trade\_no\_spontaneous\_storage} and \texttt{no\_internal\_free\_money}.
The \emph{external}/solvency half stays \emph{open}: \texttt{solvency\_of\_coverage}
keeps the coverage premise (B1/B3/B4 admissibility) as an undischarged hypothesis,
honoring \texttt{reserves\_have\_no\_floor}. We therefore present the unified
single object as a \emph{conjecture / research direction}---internal-passivity
half \tfp{}, external/solvency half open---and \emph{not} as a fully unified proven
object. (This object's potential is the Balancer constant-product potential, not a
GH cumulant-generating function.)

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The construction sits between constant-function market makers and perpetual
options. The weighted constant-product invariant is from Balancer~\cite{balancer};
the broader CFMM theory and pricing-oracle properties are
from~\cite{angeris-cfmm,angeris-uniswap}, with Uniswap v2/v3 as the
reference concentrated/constant-product
designs~\cite{uniswapv2,uniswapv3}. Panoptic~\cite{panoptic} is the closest
oracle-free perpetual-options protocol; our design differs in pricing the whole
OTM continuum from one pool state and in lifting the barrier into an American
free boundary.

The nearest analytic neighbour is the line of work treating loss-versus-rebalancing
(LVR) for an AMM as a \emph{continuum of perpetual options}~\cite{singh-lvr}:
that work reads the LP's position as a portfolio of perpetual optionality, which is
the dual of what we do---we \emph{price and sell} a perpetual-option continuum from
the same curve geometry whose LP exposure that line analyses. We flag this neighbour
explicitly for positioning; a quantitative comparison of the implied option surfaces
is left to a future version.\footnote{Full bibliographic details for the Singh et
al.\ reference are to be confirmed before submission; the entry is included here as
a placeholder per the project's must-cite record.}

The formal-methods backing rests on Lean~4~\cite{lean4} and Mathlib~\cite{mathlib};
the perpetual-American (Merton) tie connects to the classical optimal-stopping
treatment of perpetual American options.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

We state the boundaries of the present work plainly; several are restatements of
the honest-claims posture above.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Solvency is conditional.} The solvency guarantee holds only under
        the funding-port hypothesis (B1), with B3/B4 as further named hypotheses.
        Geometry is necessary (\texttt{reserves\_have\_no\_floor}) but never
        sufficient. The port is not discharged here; that it pays in practice is an
        external assumption.
  \item \textbf{Engine instantiation is not proven.} The trusted-from-prover interface stack
        is not wired to the production engine's numeric formulas; the bridge from
        the live HTML kernel to the Lean definitions is currently an oracle
        (testing) bridge, not a verified extraction. No claim that ``the HTML is
        Lean-verified'' is made.
  \item \textbf{Provenance is \tfp{}, not ``verified.''} Every Lean result is
        Aristotle-compiled and audited but not re-run against a local canonical
        kernel; the upgrade to ``verified'' awaits a local build.
  \item \textbf{Replication scope.} Exact replication holds for $|\Gamma|\le1$;
        $|\Gamma|>1$ is a labelled approximation (Section~\ref{sec:gamma}).
  \item \textbf{The unified single object is a direction.} The internal-passivity
        half is \tfp{}; the external/solvency half is open. The whole-exchange
        port-Hamiltonian object is a conjecture, not a finished proven artifact.
  \item \textbf{No measure-backed information geometry.} The kurtosis-knob's
        thermal reading is wing-scoped and non-load-bearing; only a measure-free
        Hessian geometry is available for the Balancer curve.
  \item \textbf{Single-pool rigidity.} The design exposes one directional weight
        and one rebasing frame, with no per-strike LP discretion; this rigidity is
        the source of the closed-form tractability and the structural
        no-arbitrage property, but fixes the liquidity shape up to the lens $m$.
        Calibration of funding elasticity and related parameters is
        protocol-specific and left to implementation.
\end{itemize}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

A single liquidity pool governed by the weighted constant-product invariant with
one dynamic weight prices the entire OTM perpetual-option continuum in closed
form. This draft lifts the prior barrier instrument into an American-style
perpetual option: a closed-form smooth-pasting free boundary
$S^{\ast}=K\gamma/(\gamma+1)$ with a $C^{1}$ seam and no new parameters, backed by
machine-checked (\tfp{}) settlement, boundary-uniqueness, optimality, and
Merton-tie results, and a constant slope-multiplier lens $m$ that acts as a
vol-calibrated kurtosis knob while leaving the pool flow unchanged. The no-internal-arbitrage
property is structural---a costless collar yields zero surplus iff the pool is
symmetric. The honest frontier is solvency: geometry is necessary
(\texttt{reserves\_have\_no\_floor}) but never sufficient, and the solvency
guarantee remains \emph{conditional} on a funding port that is shown necessary but
not discharged. Closing that port hypothesis---and wiring the trusted-from-prover interface
to the production engine---are the principal directions for future work.
