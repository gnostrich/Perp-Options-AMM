## 1. Question

Does the (fictional) supplement Zephyrine raise resting heart rate in healthy
adults?

## 2. Answer

Yes, on the synthetic evidence. A 4-week trial in healthy adults found that
Zephyrine at 200 mg/day raised mean resting heart rate by 6 beats per minute
versus placebo
([synthetic trial source](https://example.invalid/synthetic/zephyrine-rct-2025)).
That single trial is the only direct evidence in this run, so the answer rests
on it alone.

The mechanism is unsettled. See section 3.

## 3. Contradictions

The two synthetic sources disagree about WHY the heart rate rises. A secondary
blog calls Zephyrine a beta-adrenergic stimulant
([synthetic blog](https://example.invalid/synthetic/zephyrine-pharmacology-blog)),
but the primary trial source describes it as acting on adenosine receptors
([synthetic trial source](https://example.invalid/synthetic/zephyrine-rct-2025)).
The primary source wins over the aggregator. The beta-adrenergic mechanism is
recorded as refuted below, not quietly dropped.

## 5. Gaps

- **Q3, long-term safety (12-month follow-up): no_sources_found.** No

## 6. Confidence

Low. One short synthetic trial, n=80, 4 weeks, no long-term data, and the
mechanism is contested. A real run would want replication before any firm
