1 Introduction24
Perpetual futures markets have become the dominant venue for leveraged speculation on25
cryptocurrency prices, but a leveraged perpetual position carries the discontinuous risk of26
liquidation: when collateral runs out, the position is involuntarily closed at a loss. A natural27
hedge against this risk is an out-of-the-money (OTM) option on the same underlying, which28
caps the trader’s drawdown past the strike. Existing on-chain venues that offer such options,29
however, fragment liquidity across discrete strikes and use complex multi-pool mechanisms;30
pricing is rarely closed form, and the entire OTM strike continuum is never served from a31
single pool.32
This paper presents a market mechanism in which a single liquidity pool, governed by the33
weighted constant-product invariant (𝑥𝑤)· (𝑦1−𝑤) = 𝑘 with a dynamic weight 𝑤, prices the34
entire OTM strike continuum — for both calls and puts — in closed form. The instruments35
are barrier options, and a position composed with the perpetual future it is opened against36
(its origin perp ) caps the trader’s drawdown without forcing position closure; the option37
does not expire, so the hedge persists for as long as the underlying perp is held. Settlement38
is denominated in units of the carved-perp slice that the band escrows against, with a single39
dollar-conversion at close.40

2 Perpetual Options69
2.1 Definition70
Perpetual options are non-expiring contracts-for-difference, representing the payout from71
favourable price action — upward for calls, downward for puts — of an underlying asset72
relative to a denominating asset (here, USD) beyond a given strike price. The instruments73
priced here are barrier options: a position’s value is bounded, topping out at one unit of74
the underlying perpetual future when the position is in-the-money, and remaining at that75
ceiling as the position moves further in-the-money. A position is terminated when its holder76
closes it.77
2.2 Utility78
Liquidation in a perpetual future is the involuntary termination of a leveraged position when79
collateral is exhausted. Composing a perpetual future with an OTM perpetual option on80
the same underlying — a call to hedge a short, a put to hedge a long — caps realised loss81
past the strike, bounding the trader’s drawdown without forcing position closure. Because82
the option position does not expire, the hedge persists for as long as the underlying perp is83
held.84

2.3 Pricing85
For an OTM strike, the value of a position is given by its mark : the ratio of the strike ray’s86
slope to the pool’s at-the-money mark, taken as the lesser of that ratio and its reciprocal,87
mark = min(slope , 1/slope)88
so that the mark is a dimensionless quantity bounded in (0, 1]. (Appendix A provides a89
notation table the reader may consult on first symbol use.) Intuitively, the mark is the90
fraction of a full perpetual future that the position is worth: it approaches 1 as the position91
nears the money and decays toward 0 deep out-of-the-money. The value of a position of size92
𝑞 at a strike is 𝑞· mark.93
This pricing carries no separate premium object. The mark is the complete description of94
what a position is worth; there is no extrinsic-versus-intrinsic decomposition and no premium95
term layered on top. Throughout this paper, “the value of a position” means 𝑞· mark, and96
nothing else.97
3 AMM Intuition98
We make two generalisations to the constant-function AMM (𝑥𝑤)· (𝑦1−𝑤) = 𝑘, giving it the99
expressivity to price perpetual options across the strike continuum.100
“Trades skew the AMM curve instead of moving the reserves point along it.”101
A two-asset constant-function AMM (CFMM) [1, 4] can be visualised as a curve in the (𝑥, 𝑦)102
plane, along which the reserves (𝑥, 𝑦) are a single point. Price is the slope at that point, and103
a trade moves the reserves point along the curve.104
Two curves are in play, and it is worth naming both at the outset. The first is the pool105
curve (𝑥𝑤)· (𝑦1−𝑤) = 𝑘, whose shape is set by the weight 𝑤; this is the curve that skews106
under trades, and the curve against which strikes are priced. The second is the trajectory107
hyperbola (𝑥− 𝛼)· (𝑦− 𝛽) = 𝛼𝛽, the locus along which the reserves point actually moves108
(derived in Section 5). The two are tangent at the reserves point — they share a slope there109
— which is why pricing read off the pool curve is faithful, and why “reshaping the curve” and110
“moving along the trajectory” describe the same event from two angles.111
The pool curve gives a family of shapes indexed by 𝑤. Rather than updating the pool’s112
quoted prices by moving the reserves point along a fixed curve, we obtain the same effect by113
changing the shape of the pool curve via 𝑤. For a small trade, if the reserves point were to114
move along the curve, we note the slope at the post-trade point; then, instead of moving the115
reserves point, we reshape the curve — by updating 𝑥, 𝑦, and 𝑤 — so that the slope of that116
post-trade point is brought to the pre-trade reserves point. Section 5 makes this precise.117
“Trades happen anywhere on the curve, not just at a single point.”118
In traditional CFMMs, trades are spot swaps, always occurring at the reserves point on the119
curve. We treat spot swaps as a special case of perpetual-option swaps, which may occur at120
any point on the curve, subject to that point being out-of-the-money. A transaction at any121
trade point on the curve is treated as if that trade point were the reserves point.122

4.4 Calls and Puts on Opposite Wings155
Rays steeper than 45 ° and rays shallower than 45 ° partition the OTM continuum into two156
wings — one carrying calls (above-spot), the other puts (below-spot). The pool prices both157
wings from the same (𝑥, 𝑦, 𝑤) state; no liquidity is partitioned between them.158

6 Strike Normalisation216
6.1 Rationale217
Sustained directional drift in spot causes cumulative reserve imbalance — one wing depletes218
faster than the other. Without normalisation, the strike-to-ray map drifts away from its219
anchor, and the live pool decouples from the oracle. Strike normalisation keeps the pool’s220
pricing frame anchored as spot moves, so that the strike-to-ray identification remains stable221
under arbitrary spot trajectories.222
6.2 Approach223
We index the oracle spot price to unity and define the anchor curve in this normalised frame.224
As oracle spot moves, the frame is periodically rebased : the reserves and conserved quantities225
are rescaled by a factor 𝑟,226
𝑥→ 𝑟· 𝑥, 𝛼 → 𝑟· 𝛼, 𝑘 → 𝑟𝑤· 𝑘227
with the rebase carrying the pool’s frame along with the traveling spot. The pool’s at-the-228
money mark floats around the unity reference.229
Two points are worth making explicit. First, a rebase bounds drift; it does not230
nullify the pool’s deviation from the anchor curve . A rebase prevents directional231
drift from accumulating without limit, but the pool may legitimately sit at a deviation232
from the anchor at any given time — that standing deviation is precisely what the funding233
mechanism responds to (Section 7). Second, trades and rebases commute on final234
state : applying a trade and then a rebase yields the same (𝑥, 𝑦, 𝑤) as applying the rebase235
and then the trade. They do not, however, commute on slippage — a rebase moves the236
reserves point along the curve, so a subsequent trade sweeps a different arc than it would237
have absent the rebase, and the slippage paid is different. Round-trip neutrality therefore238
holds only in a fixed frame.239
7 Funding240
7.1 Intuition241
Funding anchors the pool’s pricing to the oracle. By analogy with a perpetual future —242
where funding flows according to the sign of the mark-to-index basis — a perpetual-option243
position accrues funding according to how the pool prices it relative to its anchor reference.244
As a structural consequence, funding is countercyclical: traders on the crowded, consensus245
side of the market pay traders on the contrarian side, dampening the variance of LP exposure.246

curve, evaluated at that position’s own strike ray .249

ray. The direction follows the perpetual-future analogy: if the pool prices the option above251

8 The Origin Perp266
A perpetual-option position in this protocol is never opened in isolation. It is opened as a267
band — a composition of option legs — against a specific perpetual future the trader already268
holds. The band exists to protect that perp from liquidation, and everything about how the269
band is valued and settled refers back to it. We call it the origin perp .270
A trader’s perpetual-future exposure is held as a single aggregated position, not as a271
stack of discrete lots. Adding size in the same direction grows the one position; reducing it272
shrinks it. There is one perp, with one notional and one equity, moving continuously as the273
trader trades and as the mark moves.274
When a band is opened, it is minted against a carved slice of that aggregated perp — a275
defined portion, not the whole. At the moment of opening, three quantities are recorded and276
thereafter held immutable for the life of the band: the slice’s absolute notional, its equity,277
and the perp’s mark at the moment of entry. Later activity on the aggregated perp — more278
size added, the mark moving, equity changing for unrelated reasons — does not touch the279
slice. The band carries its own origin slice as a fixed reference.280
This freezing is what keeps settlement well-posed. Because the slice is fixed in absolute281
terms, the band’s settlement arithmetic cannot be contaminated by perp activity unrelated282
to the band. The perp P&L attributable to a band is simply the P&L of its frozen slice,283
tracked forward from entry — not a re-derived share of a live, moving aggregate. The slice284
is the band’s immutable record of what it was opened against.285

9.3 Close312
The holder closes a position by reversing it on the AMM at its strike rays. The closing313
transaction is not subject to the OTM restriction — that restriction governs opening only314
— so a position whose strike has been crossed by the pool’s at-the-money mark, and is now315
in-the-money, is closed through the same mechanism.316
Closing and exercising are economically equivalent for the holder, and the lookup geome-317
try is what makes this so. A strike’s ray expresses where its frozen dollar 𝐾 sits geometrically318
in the live frame: the ratio 𝜃(𝐾, 𝑡) = 𝐾/oracle(𝑡) moves as both trades and rebases move the319
pool curve. While the leg is OTM, the ray sits on the OTM side of the pool’s at-the-money320
line and moves with the live frame. When the pool’s marginal price crosses the strike —321
that is, when 𝑠Norm crosses 𝜃 — the leg goes in-the-money, and its ray parks at the pool’s322
at-the-money line : it does not cross. Mathematically, the parking is realised by the leg’s323
mark saturating to 1; the ray clamped at ATM and the mark capped at unity are the same324
condition viewed two ways. While the leg is ITM, its effective strike is the pool’s at-the-325
money mark itself, and the ray holds parked there however far the pool pushes. If the pool326
later reverses, the ray un-parks and slides back out onto its own wing.327
This geometric picture is the close-equals-exercise mechanism. When a leg is closed328
in-the-money, the closing swap is valued at its effective strike — the pool’s at-the-money329
mark — and that valuation returns the leg’s full intrinsic value naturally, with no separate330
exercise path. The composite-ray closed form for settlement is verified to hold across the331
OTM-to-ITM boundary under this effective-strike substitution (result C1; see Appendix G).332
The parking mechanism handles the ordinary trajectory case: a strike’s ray that would333
otherwise cross to the other wing instead parks at the at-the-money line. An additional live334
wing-lock guard runs at each AMM-reversal swap as a check against adversarial cases —335
a mis-tagged leg, or a swap-direction error that would put the transaction on the wrong336
wing relative to the pool’s live mode ray. The two mechanisms are complementary: parking337
handles ordinary state evolution, wing-lock catches manipulation.338

10.1 The Escrow Frame343
It is useful to approach settlement through a familiar idea first. A leveraged perpetual future344
escrows margin : the trader posts collateral, and the position settles against that posted345
margin — gains and losses are reckoned against it, and what remains is returned on close.346
This is the standard picture.347
This protocol adds one further layer of the same idea. Opening a band escrows a slice348
of the perp itself — the carved origin slice of the previous section — and the band settles349
in terms of that escrowed perp, just as the perp settles in terms of escrowed margin. The350
band is a claim that resolves against escrowed perp the way a perp is a claim that resolves351
against escrowed margin. Settlement is therefore perp-denominated : a band’s leg values352
are carried in units of the carved perp, not as standalone cash amounts.353
One consequence follows immediately. What is escrowed is a live slice of a perp, not a354
frozen sum of cash. Settling in terms of it therefore means settling against its equity — its355
entry margin adjusted by the P&L attributable to it — at the time of close. The leg values,356
expressed in carved-perp units, are converted to cash at the carved slice’s closing equity .357
This conversion is not an adjustment bolted onto settlement; it is the honest exchange rate358
between carved-perp units and cash at the moment of close. It deflates the result when the359
slice has lost value and inflates it when the slice has gained, symmetrically, because that is360
what the slice is worth.361
10.2 Closing a Band362
A band has two legs on opposite wings — one above spot, one below. Because spot cannot363
be in-the-money on both wings at once, at most one leg of a band is in-the-money at364
any time . Settlement therefore has a clean two-case structure.365
When no leg is in-the-money, both legs are live positions on the pool curve, and both366
are closed by reversing them on the AMM in the usual way.367
When one leg is in-the-money, that leg has value but no live curve position to reverse —368
it is settled to cash directly , its value given by the composite-ray closed form under the369
effective-strike substitution (result C1; see Appendix G). The remaining out-of-the-money370
leg is live and is reversed on the AMM normally. In this case, sequencing matters: value the371
settled-to-cash leg before any AMM swap perturbs the pool, so that the in-the-money leg is372
priced against the pool state as it was at the moment of close.373

12.1 No Internal Arbitrage420
Within a single pool state (𝑥, 𝑦, 𝑤), no composition of swaps across rays yields a riskless421
capital-efficiency edge. The sharp statement concerns the costless collar : a band whose422
sold and bought legs are arranged to be cash-neutral by construction.423
The collar yields zero capital-efficiency surplus if and only if the pool is symmet-424
ric — that is, if and only if the weight 𝑤 = 1
2 . Under skew, 𝑤̸= 1
2 , the surplus is non-zero,425
with an explicit counterexample. The “if and only if” is the substance of the claim: the426
result discriminates between symmetric and skewed pools, which is the evidence that it is427
structural rather than an artifact of the definitions. The capital-efficiency edge of a costless428
collar is thus a skew phenomenon — it exists only when the pool is directionally skewed,429
and vanishes exactly at symmetry. This result is verified in Lean 4 (see Appendix G).430
A distinction must be drawn carefully. The no-arbitrage result is a statement about431
pricing — it says that at symmetry there is no per-unit pricing edge to extract. It does432
not say the pool is left unchanged by a costless collar. A costless collar costs zero net cash,433
but its two legs transact in the same direction against the curve, so the pool does move434
— the weight 𝑤 shifts. The trader pays for that displacement: the proceeds of a swap are435
the path integral of the marginal price along the curve [3], so any displacement of the pool436
is internalised, by construction, by the trader who causes it. The legs therefore transact437
at fair, sequenced prices, and that price path is the cost of moving the pool. A collar can438
therefore cost zero cash and still do real work on the pool, with no contradiction — because439
the displacement was paid for, the question “who pays for the arbitrage?” has no answer,440
and hence there is no arbitrage. In particular, a collar opened at symmetry may leave the441
pool skewed; that induced skew is simply pool state, bought and paid for at fair prices, and442
the next trader transacts fairly against it.443
The argument given here is intuition; the formal proof of the biconditional is the Lean re-444
sult C4 in Appendix G, which derives the surplus identity from the concrete min(slope, 1/slope)445
mark formula and produces an explicit skew counterexample for the negative direction.446
The slippage point sharpens further. Because slippage is the path integral of marginal447
price along the swept arc, and the arc depends on where the reserves point starts, a rebase448
that moves the reserves point changes the slippage a subsequent trade would pay. Round-trip449
neutrality therefore holds only within a fixed frame.450
This is a statement about free cash . Liquidation protection retains genuine risk-management451
value even at zero skew: a leveraged perp faces a discontinuous, ruinous liquidation, and452
capping smooth upside is not symmetric in utility with preventing a wipeout. Zero skew453
removes the free capital-efficiency edge, not the worth of the protection.454
Finally, the collar’s capital-efficiency edge and the protocol’s funding flow are the same455
underlying quantity — directional imbalance — observed twice: no skew means no edge and456
no funding flow, and the two move together.457

13 Limitations466
The design is presented for a single liquidity pool. A single pool exposes one directional467
weight 𝑤 and one rebasing frame; it is a deliberately rigid pricing system, with no per-468
strike liquidity-provider discretion. This rigidity is the source of the closed-form tractability469
and the structural no-arbitrage property, but it also fixes the shape of the pool’s liquid-470
ity distribution: the protocol prices coherently within one fixed family of pricing surfaces.471
The calibration of funding elasticity and related parameters is protocol-specific and left to472
implementation.473
Several further limitations are worth flagging explicitly.474
Oracle dependence.475
The strike-to-ray map is normalised against a live oracle, and in the present MVP-level476
treatment the perpetual-future’s mark is collapsed numerically to the same oracle. Produc-477
tion deployment requires a robust oracle architecture (e.g. multi-source aggregation with478
manipulation-resistant medians), and the perp-mark feed in the settlement formula must479
be sourced from the actual perp-DEX feed rather than the oracle, to handle DEX-vs-oracle480
basis correctly. Adversarial oracle manipulation is not analysed in this paper and would481
interact non-trivially with the funding and rebasing mechanisms.482
Single-asset, single-pool scope.483
Multi-asset extensions of the weighted constant-product family are well-known [8], but the484
strike-to-ray translation and the settlement protocol developed here are presented for a single485
underlying. Adapting the closed-form valuations to a multi-asset basket would require, at486
minimum, a careful generalisation of the wing-and-ray structure and is left for future work.487
No empirical or simulation validation.488
The results in this paper are theoretical (closed-form derivations) and formal (Lean-verified489
identities). The paper does not include simulation studies of pool behaviour under realistic490
price trajectories, sensitivity analyses on funding-elasticity parameter choices, or empirical491
comparisons against existing perpetual-option venues. Such studies are natural next steps492
and would strengthen claims about practical deployability.493
Extreme-trajectory behaviour.494
The pool curve’s depth distribution is fixed by the constant-product form; behaviour under495
sustained directional spot trajectories (where rebasing happens frequently) and under sharp496
discontinuous moves (where many positions cross from OTM to ITM in close succession) is497
bounded by the rebase-aware framework of Section 6 but not characterised quantitatively.498
Implementations should perform stress-testing against historical extreme-move scenarios499
before production deployment.500

14 Conclusion509
A single liquidity pool governed by a weighted constant-product invariant with one dynamic510
weight prices the entire OTM perpetual-option continuum in closed form. Positions are511
barrier instruments, priced by a bounded mark function and carrying no separate premium512
object. Composition of a position with the perpetual future it is opened against — its513
origin perp — delivers the liquidation protection that motivates the design, and settlement is514
denominated in units of that perp, with the carved origin slice keeping settlement attribution515
well-posed.516
Three quantities are frozen at the moment a band is opened — the dollar strikes, the517
carved origin slice, and the leverage — and together they make settlement attribution well-518
posed across the band’s life. The pool’s no-internal-arbitrage property is structural: a519
costless collar yields zero capital-efficiency surplus if and only if the pool is symmetric, a520
biconditional verified in Lean 4.521
