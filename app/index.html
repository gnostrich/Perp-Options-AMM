Aggregate book — what you trade against BUYSELLyour curve @ size AGGREGATEYOU
Each maker posts its own curve. The book you trade is the envelope — best ask and best bid at every strike — so the aggregate is always at least as tight as any single maker. The curve is not a line — each maker is an AMM, so taking size walks its own curve continuously and the book is a cloud: brightness is the liquidity still untouched at that price, full at the touch and fading to nothing where the book is exhausted, so how far the cloud reaches IS the depth. BUY and SELL are separate tabs; you only ever see one side. The panel below is the landing map — the aggregation is nontrivial, so the honest object is a surface landed(k,Q), not a single curve: pick a strike and a size and it shows where that trade lands you relative to the best quote. In an RFQ makers are free to differ, and buying is just the ordinary thing: you are matched to the best quote across makers. Differing is how a maker chooses a side — lean cheap and you win the ask, lean rich and you win the bid (measured: a 10% lean wins its side at 100% of strikes). If one maker's bid ends up above another's ask, that is not an arbitrage to be policed but two makers wanting opposite sides, and the venue should simply cross them. Drag maker divergence to see the sides separate.

RULED (operator, entries 554 / 568 / 569): makers differ freely, exactly as in any multi-dealer RFQ — no shared resting book, no bound imposed, fills split at each maker's own price. Put–call parity C−P=−k holds exactly on every individual maker's curve (residual 1.1e-16). A book-level parity residual can therefore only arise from min and max picking different makers — and measured, that residual and the crossed edge sum to the spread term, so they are one phenomenon counted from two sides, not a second arbitrage against the cleared perp. Buying is the ordinary thing — you are matched to the best quote across makers. A crossed book is not a defect to police: it is a maker who wants to buy quoting above a maker who wants to sell, two willing counterparties the venue should simply cross.
